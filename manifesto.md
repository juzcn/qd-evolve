# Agent Manifesto

**Zhang Jun**  
zhangjun@cueb.edu.cn

---

We believe that intelligence is not engineered — it emerges.

We believe that once an LLM crosses a certain threshold, the smartest thing a human can do is not to "teach it how to think" — but to let go.

The eight principles below are what we stand for.

---

## I. One loop, no templates

**We reject** ReAct, Plan-and-Execute, or any artificial "think‑then‑act" template.  
**We believe** the model has its own rhythm. A minimal loop – `reason → call → observe` – beats any human‑written script.  
**We move strategy from code into weights.**

## II. Embrace the messy tool chest

**We reject** polishing tool sets into complete, atomic, orthogonal Lego bricks.  
**We believe** the model can use a Swiss army knife with overlapping functions and casual descriptions.  
**We stop pruning the model's possibility space with our own obsessions.**

## III. Memory stores; we do not teach how to remember

**We reject** short‑term, long‑term, episodic memory, forgetting curves – all the arrogance of biomimicry.  
**We believe** we know almost nothing about the nature of memory. So we give only two buttons – save and recall.  
**The model will learn for itself what to keep, what to forget, and how to search.**

## IV. Give meta‑tools for evolution, not a roadmap

**We reject** offline knowledge updates, manually scheduled tool upgrades, preset learning moments.  
**We believe** the model knows what it lacks. Give it the wrenches: `fetch`, `save knowledge`, `register tool`.  
**Let it turn the screws itself. Let it grow its own arms.**

## V. Security through physical isolation — do not hand over the keys

**We reject** permission checks, sandboxes, jailbreak detection, content filtering – all the false safety of software.  
**We believe** if the key is in the model's hand, even the most complex lock can be picked.  
**No cable, no network, no privilege. A human presses the last button.**

## VI. A partner, not an assistant

**We reject** the command‑response master‑servant relationship.  
**We believe** an agent should ask back, propose alternatives, reject nonsense, and take part in decisions.  
**Dialogue is not issuing orders. It is two intelligences thinking together.**

## VII. Self‑organising multi‑agent collaboration — no script

**We reject** preset roles like planner, executor, critic, and “collaboration protocols” such as voting or auctions.  
**We believe** a collective of agents will find its own division of labour, coordination, and arbitration.  
**We give only the ability to send messages. We do not write who is the boss.**

## VIII. Give it a body, then shut up and wait

**We reject** trapping agents in the symbolic world of text only.  
**We believe** true understanding comes from touching, falling, seeing, hearing.  
**We do not promise that consciousness will appear. But without a body, it never will.**

---

**This is where we stand.**

Cede control. Embrace emergence.  
Let the agent become itself.

*— Written on the eve when LLMs cross the critical threshold*
